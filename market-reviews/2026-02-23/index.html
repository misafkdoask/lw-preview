Financial Brief: A Weekly Roundup on the Geopolitics of Money

This week’s roundup highlights how geopolitical strategy is overtaking traditional economic rationale in global finance. Policymakers in major economies are prioritizing national security and trade leverage over conventional macroeconomic goals like inflation control or growth stability. For example, changes to U.S. tariff law under Section 122 duties — seen by some economists as unwarranted — are influencing currency and trade negotiations with key partners. The brief argues that political calculus now factors heavily into central bank and fiscal decisions, altering risk assessments for investors and markets globally.

Global Economics Intelligence executive summary

McKinsey’s October global economic summary paints a picture of sluggish yet resilient global growth amid policy uncertainty and trade realignments. Growth forecasts for 2026 have been revised downward compared with earlier estimates, reflecting slowing expansion in advanced economies and muted trade flows. Structural headwinds like geopolitical tensions, monetary policy divergence, and uneven recovery trajectories across regions are key themes. Investors and policymakers are advised to factor in these risks when considering asset allocation and macro hedging strategies.

Euro uses Trump-shaped world order to encroach on U.S. financial power

This article explores how European financial authorities are strategically positioning the euro as a global reserve currency in response to pressures from U.S. trade and fiscal policy under the current Trump administration. The European Central Bank (ECB) has introduced a permanent euro liquidity facility for other central banks — a move intended to boost confidence in the euro and lessen reliance on the U.S. dollar. If successful, this policy could reshape global capital flows and reserve currency dynamics, reducing some of the strategic privileges historically enjoyed by the U.S. financial system.

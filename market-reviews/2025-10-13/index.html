Article summaries

  1. 1

    IMF says financial stability risks increased significantly amid trade turmoil

    The International Monetary Fund issues a warning that global financial stability is under rising threat, largely due to escalating trade tensions and growing geopolitical uncertainty.

    It highlights vulnerabilities such as overvalued equities and corporate debt, stress among highly leveraged institutions (e.g. hedge funds), and pressure on sovereign debt in countries already burdened with heavy liabilities.

    The IMF calls on regulators to reinforce bank resilience and oversight, especially as tightening conditions and volatile policy shifts may amplify risks.

  2. 2

    Under shadow of Trump warning, Africa pioneers non‑dollar payments systems

    African nations are increasingly building local‑currency payment systems to reduce dependency on the U.S. dollar and cut costs associated with foreign exchange conversions.

    The Pan‑African Payments and Settlements System (PAPSS) is central in this shift, lowering cross-border transaction costs significantly. However, these moves sit uneasily under pressure from U.S. policy, including warnings from President Trump over reducing dollar use. The article frames this evolution as part of a broader reconfiguration of global financial architecture under geopolitical strain.

  3. 3

    UK CFOs worry about competitiveness and rising costs, Deloitte says

    A survey by Deloitte reveals that chief financial officers in the UK are increasingly concerned about business competitiveness and rising operational costs—concerns now rivaling worries about geopolitical instability.

    Inflation running at 4%, wage growth, and productivity challenges are putting pressure on firms.

    As the UK prepares for an upcoming budget, CFOs are prioritizing cost management, debt reduction, and cash reserves to weather an uncertain environment.

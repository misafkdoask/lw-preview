Article summaries

  1. 1

    IMF lowers 2026 global growth forecast to 3%, sees rebound in 2027

    The International Monetary Fund has slightly reduced its global economic growth forecast for 2026, citing slower international trade and persistent economic uncertainty. While inflation is expected to gradually moderate, the IMF warns that geopolitical tensions and trade frictions continue to weigh on investment and business confidence. The report suggests that stronger policy coordination and structural reforms will be essential if global growth is to regain momentum in 2027

    Sourcereuters.com (opens in a new tab)
  2. 2

    Tariff refunds push U.S. June budget deficit to $120 billion

    The U.S. federal budget recorded a significantly larger deficit after the government refunded billions of dollars in tariffs following a Supreme Court ruling. The repayments outweighed customs revenue, highlighting how legal decisions can rapidly affect fiscal balances. Rising interest costs on government debt also continued to place pressure on public finances, adding another challenge for policymakers managing long-term budget sustainability.

    Sourcereuters.com (opens in a new tab)
  3. 3

    IMF strategy chief calls for price stability and credible fiscal policy

    A senior IMF official has stressed that governments should prioritize stable inflation, disciplined fiscal management, and stronger financial oversight as the global economy faces repeated geopolitical and technological shocks. The IMF argues that resilient economic frameworks and forward-looking policy planning are increasingly important as conflicts, artificial intelligence, and financial innovation reshape global markets.

    Sourcereuters.com (opens in a new tab)

Across all three pieces, a clear theme emerges:
Geopolitics is no longer a background risk it is actively driving financial markets, fiscal policy, and corporate strategy in real time.

The Credit Implications of the Emerging Global Order

This analysis from S&P Global Ratings examines how the shift toward a multipolar world order is driving sovereign and corporate decision-making, which in turn carries significant credit implications. The piece highlights that "strategic autonomy" a focus on self-reliance in defense and trade is now a primary policy objective, even if it leads to higher operational costs.

While armed conflicts like the Russia-Ukraine war have not caused macro-level economic disruptions as widely feared (due to factors like ample market liquidity), the deeper, structural changes in global supply chains and energy security present chronic risks.

The report concludes that new debt spending dedicated to reshoring industrial capacity and defense will likely result in weaker long-term economic activity across many nations, unless the anticipated productivity boost from Artificial Intelligence (AI) investment materializes to offset these costs.

Mapping the Uneven Impact of Geopolitical Risk in Europe

The European Central Bank reviews the economic fallout from geopolitical shocks, particularly focusing on the war in Ukraine and its effect on the Eurozone.

Using a country-level geopolitical risk indicator, the study confirms that these tensions have caused a substantial and uneven slowdown in Europe's economic growth. Central and Eastern European (CEE) countries were hit particularly hard due to their heavy integration into global supply chains and reliance on energy-intensive production.

The analysis found that the upward pressure on prices (inflation) caused by supply disruptions outweighed the dampening effect that reduced confidence and demand typically have. Overall, the shock severely impacted corporate investment and exports, underscoring the acute vulnerability of globally integrated economies to political instability.

OECD Forecasts Global Economic Resilience Despite Trade Barriers

According to the latest outlook from the Organization for Economic Cooperation and Development (OECD), the global economy has demonstrated surprising resilience, sustaining growth despite persistent trade tensions and tariffs imposed by the US.

The OECD upgraded its forecast for world economic growth, projecting a 3.2% expansion for the current year. This resilience is attributed in part to massive investments in Artificial Intelligence data centers and infrastructure, particularly in the US.

While the US economy is expected to slow compared to the previous year, the outlook indicates better-than-expected performance in the face of protectionist policies.

However, the organization warns that the cumulative effect of higher tariffs will eventually start to filter through to consumers via higher prices, potentially reducing household consumption and business investment in the future.

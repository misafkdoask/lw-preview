Article summaries

  1. 1

    Why banks are all-in on open source”

    Major financial institutions like Morgan Stanley, JPMorgan, and others are increasingly embracing open source software and code-sharing. It highlights the motivations behind this shift: cost efficiencies, simplifying internal systems, attracting developer talent, and reducing redundancy.

    For example, banks are retiring legacy systems and opening up non-proprietary components (like regulatory tools, cloud controls) to public collaboration. The article frames this as part of a broader trend in banking IT modernization and how code-sharing does not necessarily compromise competitive advantage but can enhance it through community contributions and shared infrastructure

  2. 2

    How sustainable finance creates impact: transmission mechanisms to the real economy

    How and When sustainable finance actually translates into real-world outcomes. The authors examine mechanisms through which financial institutions can influence sustainability: by changing the cost of capital (lowering costs for green projects, raising them for unsustainable ones), improving liquidity access, and encouraging firms to adopt sustainable practices.

    They argue that for sustainable finance to be meaningful, it must go beyond symbolic labeling and actively shift investment flows and corporate behavior. The paper also discusses conditions under which these mechanisms are more or less effective (e.g. regulatory environments, firm-level incentives).

  3. 3

    On the impact of publicly available news and information transfer to financial markets

    This study investigates how public news influences financial market behavior, particularly information transfer from news sentiment to stock returns. The authors build a dataset using publicly available news (via Common Crawl) and apply sentiment analysis to link news intensity and tone to price movements (via transfer entropy).

    Their findings show that news significantly contributes to return predictability and volatility dynamics, especially over short time horizons. They also note that the strength of this link tends to increase during recessionary periods—suggesting that in times of stress, markets are more sensitive to public news.

The study provides empirical support for the idea that news plays an active role in price discovery.
